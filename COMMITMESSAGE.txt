feat(phylo): the gene-tree step -- rooting, traits, and the R estimators

Replaces the `phylo_signal.py` stub with the step it described. The design
called a gene tree "a later option, not the default"; it is now the default,
because a species tree has one tip per taxon and this family has three, so the
paralogues would have to be averaged away before the test meant to distinguish
them.

Three new modules, each holding the part that is worth unit-testing:

* `phylo.tree` -- read a treefile, root it on a declared outgroup, reconcile
  tips with traits, check each paralogue group for monophyly, and find the
  identical tips whose traits disagree.
* `phylo.traits` -- block occupancy to a binary presence call at one named
  threshold, with the prevalence screen D and phyloglm need.
* `phylo.estimate` -- assemble the R call, and adjust the p-values that come
  back.

The estimators themselves are in `workflow/scripts/phylo_signal.R`: Fritz &
Purvis D from caper, Pagel's lambda from phytools, phylogenetic logistic
regression from phylolm. There is no Python implementation of D worth trusting,
and writing one to avoid a dependency would put the least-tested code in the
project at the point where the conclusion is drawn.

Four refusals, each put there by something that would otherwise have produced a
well-formed wrong answer:

* Biopython roots AT the outgroup MRCA, which leaves a root of degree three.
  `ape::is.rooted` is FALSE for that, and phyloglm -- which builds its
  covariance from root-to-tip paths -- refuses the tree. The rooting would have
  been performed, reported as successful, and rejected downstream. The root is
  now bifurcated with a zero-length node, and the analysis rooting is done in R
  with `ape::root(resolve.root = TRUE)`, which puts the root on the stem.
* A paraphyletic outgroup is refused rather than rooted through. Its MRCA holds
  ingroup tips, so rooting there moves them across the root -- and the
  disagreement between the declaration and the tree is a finding, not a detail
  to route around.
* An outgroup made of two children of an unrooted trifurcation has the root as
  its MRCA. An MRCA-only monophyly check calls that paraphyletic; it is not, and
  the complement is checked too. IQ-TREE writes exactly this shape.
* The alignment is NOT deduplicated. Identical rows give zero-length branches,
  which look like something to clean up and are not: IQ-TREE collapses them for
  the search and restores them, whereas deduplicating keeps one member and
  discards the others' traits. On the FXR1 trimmed alignment 33 groups of
  identical rows cover 90 of 372 sequences -- only 7 of them identical before
  trimming -- and 11 hold members whose APR masks differ, by up to 84 residues.
  `trait_conflicts` measures that instead of deleting it.

Two things the step deliberately does not do. The paralogue group is not a
regression predictor: each group descends from one duplication, so a group
contrast rests on two independent events however many tips it spans, and
phyloglm would compute a coefficient as if all 282 were data -- the same
pseudoreplication §4 exists to prevent, one level up. The controlled instrument
is the paired within-taxon contrast. And the permutation floor is checked rather
than trusted: a permutation p cannot fall below 1/(k+1), BH at alpha over m
blocks cannot reject unless some p reaches alpha/m, and this project has already
shipped a table of all-null results produced by exactly that arithmetic going
unchecked.

Also: a mostly-gap tip is dropped BEFORE inference, in `rule tree_input`, since
absent characters do not constrain the topology and pruning the tip from a
finished tree does not undo its effect on the neighbours' branch lengths. 15 of
372 FXR1 sequences fall below 0.5 occupancy.

`phylo_targets` asked for `results/summary/phylo_signal.tsv`, which no rule
produced; it is now per-protein and gated on a paralogue split and an outgroup
both being configured.

ete3 and dendropy are dropped from the phylo extra in favour of biopython:
read, reroot, MRCA, monophyly and prune are operations Biopython already has,
and ete3 pulls a GUI stack for them.

21 new tests (248 passing in all). Verified on the FXR1 data through to the R
boundary: 15 tips below occupancy, 57 of 58 outgroup tips surviving the filter,
13 of 15 blocks testable at threshold 0.5 (12 at 0.4, 13 at 0.6), the two
refusals being the near-universal blocks at 98.6% and 95.7% prevalence -- no
variation there for a tree to explain, which is a limit of the question rather
than a threshold to tune.
