feat(viz): taxonomic tracks for iTOL, and a rooting that actually realised the split

Two tracks were missing and one bug was hiding behind a check that passed.

TAXONOMY. `io.annotate.major_groups` collapses a kingdom-spanning taxonomy into
groups a legend can hold. Neither rank works alone on this set: `class` gives 25
values of which 11 have under three members, `phylum` gives 12 but puts every
vertebrate in one bucket. So ranks are tried finest to coarsest and a group is
kept the first time it reaches min_size -- with the remainder RE-COUNTED among
itself, which is the part that matters. Falling back to phylum for three
coelacanths must not produce "Chordata", a name already covering the 217 mammals
assigned a rank earlier; counting only what is still unassigned turns it into
"Chordata (other)" and the suffix says so. The FXR1 set yields nine groups.

Those go out as DATASET_SYMBOL (a shape per group, in an external column) and
DATASET_COLORSTRIP (the same as a band). Not as clade ranges, and that is a
statement about gene trees rather than a shortcut: a taxonomic group appears
once inside EACH paralogue clade, so "Mammalia" is not a clade and the MRCA of
its 206 tips is very nearly the whole tree. `category_styles` varies shape
before colour, so the first six groups differ in both -- shape survives
greyscale and colour blindness, colour alone does not.

PARALOGUE CLADES now draw as ONE range each at their MRCA, via
`phylo.tree.mrca_pair`, which names an internal node the way iTOL does: two
leaves joined by a bar. 357 per-leaf ranges paint 357 thin stripes and hide
where a clade starts; four ranges paint four blocks. mrca_pair returns None for
a group that is not a clade rather than approximating one, and the per-leaf form
stays as the fallback.

THE BUG. Building those ranges is what exposed it. `root_on_outgroup` reported
the 57-tip outgroup monophyletic -- correctly, it is one side of a valid split --
and then produced a tree whose root separated 2 tips from 355. Biopython roots
AT the MRCA of its targets, and on a tree IQ-TREE wrote as a trifurcation the
outgroup spans the root, so its MRCA is the whole tree and rooting there
scatters it. The fix roots on whichever side is actually a clade, the outgroup
or its complement, and then VERIFIES that the root separates the two sets
instead of trusting that it did.

The published D and lambda are unaffected: the R side re-roots independently
with ape::root(resolve.root = TRUE), which gets this right, and the per-clade
subtrees are taken by keep.tip. What was wrong was the Python-side tree used for
clade checks and, now, for clade ranges -- where pre-duplication came back "not
monophyletic" one line after the rooting had called it monophyletic. That
contradiction is what a verification step is for.

Also documented, because it is silent: viz.groups.colour_for assigns the palette
by position in the SORTED group names, so a dataset built from a subset of the
groups comes out in colours that do not match the figures. Sharing the function
is necessary and not sufficient; callers must pass every group.

322 tests passing.
