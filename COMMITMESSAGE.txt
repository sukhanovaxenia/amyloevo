fix(phylo): every D failed on a label check, and lambda answered a foregone question

Three problems, all of which produced a complete, well-formatted table.

1. caper refused the tree and D came back empty for all 15 blocks.
   comparative.data checks duplicated(c(tip.label, node.label)) -- one combined
   vector -- and IQ-TREE writes branch support into the internal node labels.
   126 of the 330 node labels on the FXR1 tree are repeated ("100/100" eleven
   times), so every phylo.d call died with "Labels duplicated between tips and
   nodes in phylogeny" while lambda and phyloglm, which never read node labels,
   ran fine. The result was a D column of nothing with no sign that the cause
   was a label check rather than the data. The support values are read off the
   treefile on the Python side and reported there, so they are now dropped
   before comparative.data, with a message saying how many and why.

2. lambda over the whole gene tree asks a question whose answer is settled in
   advance. The deepest split in this tree IS the duplication, and the three
   paralogue groups carry different APR profiles, so "is this trait
   non-independent on this tree" has to come back yes -- and it did, with
   lambda between 0.75 and 1.00 on all 15 blocks and likelihood-ratio p-values
   down to 0.0 and 7.16e-320.

   Every block is now screened and tested inside each clade as well as over the
   whole tree, and the two are kept apart everywhere: separate sections in the
   report, separate BH families in `adjust` (pooling them would inflate m
   four-fold for no gain in error control), a `scope` column in the table.
   Prevalence inside a clade is not prevalence overall, which is the point: a
   block carried by 95% of everything can be at 50% inside one group, and on
   this family most blocks turn out to be universal or absent WITHIN a
   paralogue group and only variable between them. That is a result the
   whole-tree rows cannot state.

3. Five blocks shared a lambda of 0.999926686256414 -- twelve identical digits
   -- and all fifteen a phyloglm alpha of 1.51447 to six figures. Independent
   fits to different traits do not agree to that precision; those are
   optimiser bounds reported as estimates. `boundary_warnings` now flags a
   lambda at the top of its scale, lambdas tied to nine significant digits, and
   alpha on a search bound -- taking phyloglm's own `alphaWarn` when it is
   available rather than inferring it from ties -- and the R script records
   alphaWarn and convergence per fit. A p-value below 1e-300 is printed as
   "<1e-300" instead of as 0.0, because "p = 0" is not a sentence to put in a
   manuscript.

   R's warnings are also summarised at the end of the script. "There were 16
   warnings" in a log nobody expands is the same as silence.

Separately, `itol_annotations.py` is no longer a stub. Five datasets for the
gene tree -- block presence (DATASET_BINARY), paralogue colours (TREE_COLORS),
readable leaf labels (LABELS), and mean block occupancy as both a gradient and
bars. Two decisions worth the words: a tip with no block profile gets -1
(unknown), not 0, since 75 of the 357 FXR1 tips failed the completeness filter
and drawing them as lacking every block would be an invented result; and the
palette comes from viz.groups.colour_for rather than a second hand-keyed dict,
so a paralogue cannot be one colour in a figure and another on the tree.

iTOL silently drops any row whose label is not a leaf, so a dataset built from
the wrong id column uploads cleanly and renders an empty track -- which reads as
"no APRs in this family". `check_tips` compares every dataset against the tree's
tips and the script logs the counts.
