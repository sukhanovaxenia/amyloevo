init: amyloevo -- evolutionary conservatism of APRs across orthologues

Scaffold plus the three modules that need no external tool, so the parts where a
silent error would corrupt every downstream number are written and tested before
anything depends on them.

The question, and why it needs two measures

  Whether APR positions are conserved is not the question. Conserved positions
  are conserved for many reasons, and an APR inside a conserved domain reports on
  the domain rather than on amyloidogenicity. What would distinguish selection on
  the PROPERTY is a column whose residues turn over while the amyloidogenic call
  persists -- and that is only visible as a residual between sequence
  conservation and APR conservation. Both are therefore measured, and the
  residual is the result. docs/DESIGN.md carries the argument in full.

project/columns.py -- the coordinate spine

  Every conservation measure is per alignment column; every prediction is per
  residue of an individual sequence. Nothing else in the package converts between
  them by hand.

  Three properties make it worth isolating. The offset is NOT constant: a gap
  displaces every later residue in that sequence alone, so "add the gap count at
  the start of the region" mislocates the region's end, and a test pins that with
  a gap placed inside KLVFFA. The mapping is partial in both directions, and the
  missing case returns None rather than 0 or -1, because a sentinel invites
  arithmetic on it. And the numbering is 1-based on both axes, matching the
  predictors and every alignment viewer a reader will check against; the crossing
  to 0-based indices happens once, here.

  columns_for_region and region_span answer different questions -- the residues'
  own columns, and the span a viewer would highlight, interior gaps included --
  so neither is the other's default.

  transfer() returns None where the orthologue is gapped. That is the meaningful
  answer rather than a failure: the orthologue has no residue there, and an APR
  cannot be conserved at a position that does not exist.

conserve/scores.py -- entropy, JSD, APR fraction, residual

  Gaps are excluded from a column's distribution rather than treated as a 21st
  character: a gap is missing evidence, not a conserved residue, and counting it
  makes an unobserved column look variable.

  JSD is measured against real Swiss-Prot background frequencies, not a uniform
  one. A column of pure tryptophan is more surprising than a column of pure
  leucine; entropy calls both zero and only a background distribution can tell
  them apart. A test pins exactly that pair.

  Sparsely occupied columns are returned UNDEFINED rather than scored. Three
  residues out of two hundred give entropy near zero whatever they are, so
  without the gate every insertion would enter the analysis as a maximally
  conserved site -- the inverse of the truth.

  apr_residual() regresses APR conservation on sequence conservation and returns
  what is left. An undefined fit returns no residuals rather than zeros, since
  zero reads as "fully explained by sequence", which is the opposite of "not
  measurable".

  One test was written wrong and is worth recording. It checked the residual on a
  balanced 2x2 of (conserved/variable) x (APR/no APR), which makes the two
  variables uncorrelated BY CONSTRUCTION: the slope collapses to whatever the
  background frequencies leave behind (-0.38 here, from G scoring 0.81 against
  L's 0.76), and the ordering of the residuals became an artefact rather than a
  property of the metric. The residual is defined against a fitted trend, so the
  test now establishes a trend first and probes it. A second test pins the
  degenerate regime explicitly: with no trend, the residual reduces to a centred
  apr_fraction and must not be read as conservation-corrected.

io/headers.py -- EggNOG, UniProt, NCBI

  Strict by default: one unreadable header raises, naming the format tried and
  the first few offenders. An orthologue with no taxon cannot be placed on the
  species tree, so quietly analysing whichever headers matched would produce a
  phylogenetic test run on a subset nobody chose, with no recoverable
  denominator.

What is specified but not written

  align (MAFFT/MUSCLE), predict (the aggressor-wrappers panel over orthologues),
  phylo (phylogenetic signal and regression), viz (logos, MEME export). Each
  needs an external tool, which is exactly why the pure modules came first.

  Two constraints recorded in DESIGN.md before any of that is built. Five of the
  twelve predictors have no local arm, so an orthologue sweep of 10^4-10^5
  sequences must run the seven-tool local panel -- and the reference proteins
  must be RE-RUN through that same panel, or every orthologue appears to have
  lost APRs that were never tested for. And the phylogenetic question cannot be
  answered by correlation: species share ancestry, so it needs Fritz & Purvis's D
  or Pagel's lambda for signal, and phylogenetic logistic regression for the
  dependency.

workflow/ -- Snakemake owns the running, the package owns the logic

  Counting the external programs (MAFFT/MUSCLE, MMseqs2/CD-HIT, IQ-TREE, the
  predictor panel) against 372 proteins gives on the order of a thousand jobs with
  a real dependency graph and partial failures. That is a workflow manager's job,
  and a Python package driving it with subprocess loops would be a worse
  Snakemake. But a Snakemake rule is not unit-testable, and the steps where being
  quietly wrong is possible -- column mapping, the occupancy gate, the residual's
  undefined case -- are exactly the ones needing tests. So rules stay thin: read
  inputs, call one library function, write outputs.

  Five scripts are implemented (reference lookup, alignment metadata, per-column
  conservation, the run-level fit, provenance) and five are stubs that raise with
  what they must produce, so --dryrun reports the whole graph rather than a missing
  input and the remaining work is written where it happens.

  Three config choices carry scientific consequences and are enforced rather than
  documented:

  Redundancy is corrected ONCE. Clustering and phylogenetic weighting address the
  same bias -- densely sampled clades counting many times in a per-column fraction
  -- so method: cluster together with cluster.cap_only: true is rejected at parse
  time. Clustering to bound compute and clustering to fix sampling are different
  claims about the result, and the provenance records which was made.

  The aligner and its arguments are written beside every alignment with a
  checksum. Column assignments move with the aligner and the residual is a
  difference of two column-dependent terms, so this is part of the result rather
  than a log line.

  predict.from_intervals skips prediction entirely, dropping predict_panel and
  apr_calls from the DAG -- and with them the residual, which needs a call per
  orthologue. The reduced mode is legitimate and its cost is explicit.

  The residual is fitted ONCE at run level, not per protein: a few hundred columns
  give a slope with no stability. The slope itself is an output, and
  residual_fit.yaml states in words whether the residual means anything -- near
  zero, it collapses to a centred APR fraction and is not conservation-corrected.

  Verified: the DAG resolves to 14 jobs for one protein, and the four switches that
  should change it do (clustering adds a job, from_intervals removes two,
  tree: none removes three, muscle replaces mafft). Both guards fire -- the
  double-correction refusal and the empty-input refusal. conserve.py and
  summarise.py were run end to end on a three-orthologue group in which one
  sequence carries a two-residue gap before its APR: its residues 16-18 land in
  alignment columns 18-20, and the table shows apr_fraction 1.0 at those columns
  against 0.667 at 16, 17 and 21. Matching residue numbers instead of columns would
  have put the agreement in the wrong place, which is the whole reason the
  coordinate spine exists.

54 tests, no external tool required to run them.
