fix(conserve): a per-level weight ranking that was one number five times

summarise() sums weights per taxonomic level and printed the five heaviest.
Under by_rank at that same rank every level sums to n/k by construction, so
the list was 5.64 printed five times under five different order names, read
as a ranking of orders when there was nothing to rank. On the FXR1 set it
announced Cichliformes, Chiroptera, Carnivora, Diptera and Rodentia as the
"heaviest orders" -- which invites precisely the wrong conclusion about which
clades the weighting favours, since by_rank(order) favours the SMALLEST
orders and those five are simply the first five of sixty-six equal totals.

The degenerate case is now named instead of listed, and the real ranking
branch reports a share of the total weight rather than a bare sum, so the two
schemes' output is comparable side by side. Added the heaviest single sequence
and its share of the total: on the FXR1 set Henikoff puts 7.4% of all weight
on one sequence, which is the number a reader needs in order to distrust it.

Three tests. One of them needed its fixture rewritten before it could fail at
all: under Henikoff three identical rodents collectively weigh exactly what
one fly does, so a 3-vs-1 split comes out equal per order and would have
passed whatever summarise() printed. Unequal numbers of DISTINCT sequences
per level is what makes the ranking branch reachable.

348 tests pass.
